# Comment Engagement System — Christine Laikind
### Brand Foundation + Content Engine + Get Found Audit
### ICP: Bookkeeping firm owners & solopreneur bookkeepers, $250K–$1M revenue

---

## 1. Comment Reply Framework

**(a) Someone agreeing/relating**
> Acknowledge specifically what they related to, add one small piece of insight they didn't mention, then invite continuation.
> Example: *"That 'too busy being good at the job to be found for it' feeling — I hear it from almost every bookkeeping firm owner I talk to. Curious what it's looked like for you — has it been a slow fade or more of a sudden drop-off?"*

**(b) Someone sharing their own experience**
> Reflect back the specific detail they shared (not a generic "thanks for sharing!"), then ask a follow-up that naturally continues in DM.
> Example: *"A year and a half without a new referral is a long stretch — especially when the work itself hasn't changed. Mind if I ask what you've tried so far to fix it? I might be able to point you somewhere useful."*

**(c) Someone asking a question**
> Answer the actual question briefly and usefully in the comment (never "DM me for the answer" — that reads as gatekeeping), then offer to go deeper in DM if they want it.
> Example: *"Good question — short answer: it's usually the About section, not the headline, doing the most damage. Happy to point out exactly what I mean on yours if you want to send me your profile link."*

**(d) Someone mildly disagreeing**
> Don't get defensive or over-explain. Validate the part that's fair, hold your actual point, and invite the conversation to continue — disagreement is often the start of your best conversations, not something to smooth over.
> Example: *"Fair pushback — referrals absolutely still matter, I'm not saying drop them. The problem is when they're the only channel and one dries up. What's your mix looked like lately?"*

## 2. DM Opener Templates

**1.**
> Hey [Name] — saw your comment on my post about the referral slowdown. The "sudden drop-off vs. slow fade" thing you mentioned is actually really common — which was it for you?

**2.**
> Hey [Name], thanks for engaging on that post. You mentioned you've tried posting more without much traction — I'm curious what that actually looked like, if you don't mind sharing. Might help me understand what's missing.

**3.**
> [Name] — appreciated the honest pushback in the comments. Genuinely curious what your current client mix looks like between referrals and everything else. No pitch, just trying to understand where firms like yours actually are right now.

**4.**
> Hey [Name], following up on the self-assessment — before I send it over, is your firm doing roughly $250K–$1M right now? Want to make sure the questions actually land for where you're at.

**5.**
> [Name] — your comment about not knowing what a competitor's doing differently stuck with me. That's usually the exact moment firms realize it's a visibility problem, not a work problem. What made you notice it?

## 3. The Bridge — Example DM Exchanges

**Exchange 1 (from comment type a — relating)**

> **Christine:** That "too busy being good at the job to be found for it" feeling — I hear it constantly. Has it been a slow fade for you, or more sudden?
>
> **Prospect:** Honestly a slow fade. I didn't even notice until this year when I realized I hadn't gotten a new referral since January.
>
> **Christine:** That's the pattern I see most — it's quiet until suddenly it isn't. Have you tried anything to address it yet, or has it mostly been "I know I should but haven't gotten to it"?
>
> **Prospect:** Mostly the second one, honestly. I know I need to post more but I don't even know where to start.
>
> **Christine:** That's actually really common — and usually it's not a "post more" problem, it's a "no system" problem. I put together a quick self-assessment that shows exactly where the gap is for firms like yours — want me to send it over?

**Exchange 2 (from comment type c — question)**

> **Christine:** Good question — short answer: it's usually the About section doing the damage, not the headline. Want me to take a quick look at yours?
>
> **Prospect:** Sure, here's my profile: [link]
>
> **Christine:** [Specific, genuine observation about their actual profile] — that's a really common pattern, by the way, not just you. If you want the full picture of what's working and what's not, that's basically what my Get Found Audit does — want me to walk you through what that looks like?

**Exchange 3 (from comment type b — sharing experience)**

> **Christine:** A year and a half without a new referral is a long stretch. What have you tried so far?
>
> **Prospect:** I hired someone to redo my website but it hasn't really changed anything.
>
> **Christine:** That's really common actually — a lot of firms fix the website before fixing the positioning underneath it, so the new site ends up saying the same unclear thing, just prettier. Would it help to get a clear read on what's actually missing before you spend more on the visual side?
>
> **Prospect:** Yeah, honestly that would help.
>
> **Christine:** I do a Get Found Audit that's built exactly for this — a real diagnosis before you fix anything else. Want me to send you what's included?

## 4. What Not To Do

1. **Don't skip the real conversation to pitch early.** The moment a DM turns into an unprompted offer pitch before genuine back-and-forth has happened, trust drops and replies stop. Earn at least 2-3 real exchanges before introducing the Audit.

2. **Don't send generic openers that ignore their specific comment.** "Thanks for engaging! Let me know if you have questions" reads as a template and gets ignored — always reference the specific thing they said.

3. **Don't ask for the sale before you've asked a real question.** The strongest transitions in the examples above all lead with curiosity about their situation before ever mentioning the Audit — reversing that order (pitch first, question second) is the fastest way to go cold.
