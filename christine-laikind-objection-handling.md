# Objection Handling Matrix — Christine Laikind
### Brand Foundation + Content Engine + Get Found Audit ($950 / $1,750 / $2,250 / $4,500 bundle)
### ICP: Bookkeeping firm owners & solopreneur bookkeepers, $250K–$1M revenue

---

## 1. "I don't have the budget right now"

**Real concern:** Usually either genuine cash-flow timing, or the value hasn't been made concrete enough yet relative to the cost — bookkeepers think in numbers, so a vague "it's worth it" won't land.

**Response framework:** Don't discount. Instead, translate the cost into terms they already think in — cost of one new client vs. investment, or cost of staying stuck vs. one-time investment. Then offer the lowest-friction entry point (the $950 Audit) as a way to start without the full commitment.

**Example response:**
> Totally fair — and honestly, that's exactly why the Audit exists at $950 instead of jumping straight to the full system. It's a real starting point, not a scaled-down version of the pitch. What's one new client worth to your firm over a year? For most firms in your range that's well past $950 — this pays for itself with one new client, not a full year of them.

---

## 2. "I need to think about it"

**Real concern:** Often a stall covering a specific unstated hesitation (price, trust, timing, "will this actually work for me specifically") rather than a genuine need for more time.

**Response framework:** Don't push past it — get curious about what "thinking about it" actually means. Naming the real hesitation directly is more respectful than pretending the stall is really about time.

**Example response:**
> Of course, makes sense. Can I ask — is it more that you want time to think it through, or is there something specific giving you pause? I'd rather know now than have you disappear and wonder.

---

## 3. "I've tried something like this before and it didn't work"

**Real concern:** Genuine skepticism from a real bad experience — likely a generic agency, a DIY attempt with no strategy, or advice that wasn't specific to bookkeeping. This is a real, valid concern, not a smokescreen.

**Response framework:** Don't dismiss the past attempt. Get specific about what they tried, then draw a clear, honest distinction between that and this — without trashing whoever they worked with before.

**Example response:**
> That's really common, actually — what did you try before, if you don't mind me asking? [Listen] That makes sense why it didn't stick. A lot of what's out there is generic small-business marketing advice that isn't built around how bookkeeping firms actually get clients. This is built specifically for that — different starting point, not just a nicer version of the same thing.

---

## 4. "I don't have time to implement this on top of client work"

**Real concern:** Legitimate, and specific to this ICP — they're often still billable and protective of their time, since time literally is money in their business model.

**Response framework:** Directly address the time cost, since this is your actual differentiator (a system, not a DIY content grind). Be specific about what's required of them vs. what isn't.

**Example response:**
> That's the exact problem this is built to solve, actually — not "here's a strategy, go implement it yourself for hours every week." The Content Engine specifically exists so you're not the one generating content from scratch every week. What would actually be required of your time is [be specific: e.g. "about an hour upfront, then periodic check-ins"] — not an ongoing time drain.

---

## 5. "How is this different from [competitor/DIY approach]?"

**Real concern:** Wants to know if this is generic marketing dressed up for their industry, or genuinely built for bookkeepers specifically.

**Response framework:** Lead with your specific credibility (marketing exec + 3x business owner who noticed this exact gap in bookkeeping firms) and the specificity of the system (Audit → Brand Foundation → Content Engine, mapped to how bookkeeping firms actually grow) — not a generalized comparison to competitors by name.

**Example response:**
> Fair question. Most marketing help out there is built for small businesses broadly, not bookkeeping firms specifically — which means you get generic advice that technically applies but doesn't actually address how your clients find you or what makes them trust a bookkeeper specifically. I spent years as a marketing exec before running three businesses myself, and built this system specifically around the gap I kept seeing in bookkeeping firms — not adapted from a generic template.

---

## 6. "I want to talk to my [spouse/business partner] first"

**Real concern:** Sometimes genuine, sometimes a soft stall. The key is distinguishing which, and making it easy for the real conversation to happen productively rather than getting lost.

**Response framework:** Support it if genuine, and offer something concrete to bring to that conversation rather than leaving them to explain it from memory.

**Example response:**
> Totally understand, that makes sense for a decision like this. Want me to send you a short summary of what we discussed so you're not trying to explain it all from memory? Happy to answer any questions that come up on their end too, if that would help.

---

## 7. "Can you just tell me the price first?"

**Real concern:** Sometimes genuine budget-checking, sometimes a way to end the conversation quickly if there's no real interest yet. Bookkeepers in particular tend to want numbers up front — respect that instinct rather than dodging it.

**Response framework:** Don't be evasive — bookkeepers especially will read hedging as a red flag. Give the range immediately, then offer the entry point.

**Example response:**
> Sure — the full system runs $4,500, but you don't have to start there. The Get Found Audit is $950 and stands on its own as a real starting point. Happy to break down what's in each if that's useful.

---

## General Principles for This Audience

1. **Give real numbers fast.** Bookkeepers are detail-oriented and numbers-driven — vague answers or reluctance to state a price reads as evasive to this specific audience more than most.

2. **Distinguish stalls from genuine objections by asking, not guessing.** A direct, respectful "is it more X or Y?" question gets more honest information than assuming and responding to the wrong concern.

3. **Skepticism toward "marketing" is earned, not irrational.** Many bookkeeping firm owners have been burned by generic agencies before — meet that skepticism with specificity (what exactly is different, what exactly is included) rather than reassurance alone.
