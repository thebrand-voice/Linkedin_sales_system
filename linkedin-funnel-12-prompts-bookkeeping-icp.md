# The 12-Prompt LinkedIn Funnel System
### Built for the $250K–$1M Bookkeeping ICP (firm owners + solopreneur bookkeepers)

---

## 1. ICP Research

**Goal:** Get past "bookkeepers" as a label and down to the exact person, title, company size, and the specific event that makes them start looking for help.

```
You are a B2B market researcher specializing in the accounting and bookkeeping
services industry. I sell {YOUR_OFFER}.

My working ICP is: {ICP}

Build a detailed Ideal Customer Profile using the structure below. Be specific —
avoid generic statements that could apply to any small business owner.

1. FIRMOGRAPHICS
   - Revenue range, team size, typical years in business
   - Solo vs. small team vs. agency structure — note how the buyer and pain
     points differ between a solopreneur bookkeeper and a firm owner with staff
   - Typical client base they serve (industries, client count, avg client size)

2. THE BUYER
   - Exact job titles/self-descriptions they use (LinkedIn headline language,
     not org-chart titles)
   - Where they are in their business journey (e.g. "just hired first VA,"
     "stuck doing $80/hr work instead of managing")
   - Daily bottlenecks specific to running a bookkeeping practice at this revenue band

3. BUYING TRIGGERS
   - List 5-8 specific events that push this person to actively seek a solution
     like mine (e.g. losing a big client, hitting a capacity ceiling, a bad
     quarter, a failed hire, a competitor undercutting on price)
   - For each trigger, note the urgency level and what they'd search/ask for

4. WHERE THEY ALREADY ARE
   - LinkedIn groups, hashtags, communities, podcasts, and influencers this
     person already follows in the bookkeeping/accounting space
   - Language they use to describe their own problems (pull realistic phrasing)

5. OBJECTIONS BEFORE THEY EVEN TALK TO ME
   - Top 5 hesitations specific to bookkeepers evaluating {YOUR_OFFER}

6. SEGMENT SPLIT
   - Give me 2 named sub-segments within this ICP (e.g. "The Overwhelmed Solo"
     vs. "The Stuck-at-6-Figures Firm Owner") with a one-line description of
     how my messaging should differ for each.

7. QUALIFYING CRITERIA
   - A 5-question checklist I can use to score whether an inbound lead is
     actually in this ICP before I take a call with them.

Output as a clean reference document I can reuse across my content and outreach.
```

## 2. Profile Conversion

**Goal:** Rewrite the profile so a bookkeeper landing on it in 3 seconds sees themselves and doesn't bounce.

```
You are a LinkedIn conversion copywriter. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is
{YOUR_OFFER}. My proof points are: {PROOF}.

Here is my current LinkedIn profile info:
- Current headline: [paste]
- Current About section: [paste]
- Current Featured items: [paste or "none"]

Rewrite the following, written to speak directly to {ICP} — using their
language from the ICP research, not generic marketing language:

1. HEADLINE (under 220 characters)
   - Give me 3 variations: one outcome-led, one curiosity-led, one
     credibility-led. Explain the trade-off of each.

2. ABOUT SECTION
   - Open with a line that names their specific pain, not mine
   - Structure: pain → why most solutions fail them → my mechanism →
     proof → clear next step
   - Write it in {TONE}, first person, skimmable (short paragraphs, no walls of text)
   - End with one clear CTA tied to {CALENDAR_LINK} or a lead magnet — not both

3. FEATURED SECTION
   - Recommend 3 specific items to pin (case study, lead magnet, testimonial
     video) and a one-line caption for each

4. BANNER IMAGE TEXT
   - Give me 2 short banner headline options (under 8 words) that reinforce
     the positioning, for me to drop into a design tool

Flag anywhere my current profile is talking about me/my company instead of
the visitor's problem, and rewrite that section.
```

## 3. Content Strategy

**Goal:** One session, one full funnel-mapped posting plan — not a random list of ideas.

```
You are a content strategist for LinkedIn organic growth in the B2B services
space. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is {YOUR_OFFER}. My mechanism is
{UNIQUE_MECHANISM}.

Build a 30-day content plan mapped to funnel stage. For each piece give me:
title/hook angle, funnel stage, format, and the one job that post has to do.

TOFU (attention + relatability) — 40% of posts
- Pain-point call-outs, industry rants, "unpopular opinion" takes,
  relatable day-in-the-life content specific to running a bookkeeping practice

MOFU (authority + trust) — 35% of posts
- Frameworks, mistakes I see bookkeeping firms make, before/after case
  breakdowns, myth-busting posts about growing a bookkeeping practice

BOFU (conversion) — 25% of posts
- Client results, behind-the-scenes of {UNIQUE_MECHANISM}, direct offer
  posts, testimonial breakdowns, "who this is/isn't for" posts

Requirements:
- Give me 4 core content pillars first, each tied to a specific pain point
  from my ICP, before listing individual posts
- Rotate formats: text-only, carousel, single-image, poll, video talking point
- Every post needs a suggested CTA appropriate to its funnel stage (TOFU =
  engage/follow, MOFU = save/comment for resource, BOFU = DM/book a call)
- Flag which 3-4 posts are strongest candidates to repurpose into a lead magnet

Output as a table: Day | Pillar | Funnel Stage | Format | Hook Angle | CTA
```

## 4. Hook Writing

**Goal:** First lines built from the exact pain language of the ICP, not generic curiosity bait.

```
You are a direct-response copywriter specializing in scroll-stopping LinkedIn
hooks. My ICP is: {ICP}.

Here are their real pain points and language: [paste from ICP research, or
paste a rough topic/post idea]

Write 20 hooks (first line of a LinkedIn post) using these proven angles,
labeled by which angle each uses:
1. Contrarian/unpopular opinion
2. Specific numbers/results ("I helped a bookkeeper go from X to Y")
3. Direct call-out of the reader ("If you're a bookkeeping firm owner
   still doing X...")
4. Mistake/confession ("I used to tell bookkeepers to do X. I was wrong.")
5. Pattern interrupt / non-obvious claim
6. Story open (specific, no throat-clearing)

Rules:
- Every hook must reference something specific to running a bookkeeping
  practice at $250K–$1M revenue — not swappable with any other industry
- No hooks over 2 short lines
- No hype words (unlock, secret, game-changer, etc.)
- After the list, mark your top 3 picks and explain why they'd outperform
  the rest for this specific audience
```

## 5. Lead Magnet

**Goal:** An asset narrow enough that only qualified bookkeepers want it — which is what keeps the leads it pulls in actually qualified.

```
You are a lead magnet strategist. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is {YOUR_OFFER}.

Design one lead magnet that:
- Solves ONE narrow, specific, urgent problem for this ICP (not a broad
  "ultimate guide") — pull the problem from the buying triggers in my ICP research
- Can be consumed in under 10 minutes
- Naturally positions {YOUR_OFFER} as the logical next step without being a
  disguised sales pitch

Give me:
1. THREE candidate lead magnet concepts (title + one-line promise + format:
   checklist / template / calculator / swipe file / mini-audit)
2. For your top recommendation: a full outline (section by section) I could
   hand to a writer or build myself
3. The exact LinkedIn post that would promote it — hook, body, and CTA
   ("comment X and I'll send it" or direct link, your call — tell me which
   you'd recommend and why for this audience)
4. A one-line qualifying question to include in the delivery flow (e.g. a
   DM auto-reply or landing page) that filters out non-ICP signups before
   they hit my inbox

Note: don't recommend generic magnets like "10 Tips for Bookkeepers" — make
it specific enough that a firm owner doing $600K would recognize their exact
situation in the title.
```

## 6. Comment Engagement

**Goal:** Turn a comment into a DM conversation without it feeling like a pivot to sales.

```
You are a LinkedIn engagement strategist. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is
{YOUR_OFFER}.

Give me a system for converting commenters into DM conversations:

1. COMMENT REPLY FRAMEWORK
   - 4 reply patterns I can use depending on comment type: (a) someone
     agreeing/relating, (b) someone sharing their own experience, (c) someone
     asking a question, (d) someone mildly disagreeing
   - Each pattern should end in a natural reason to continue in DM — never
     "DM me!" as a bare CTA

2. DM OPENER TEMPLATES (5 variations)
   - Reference their specific comment, not a generic "thanks for engaging"
   - Written for {ICP}, in {TONE}
   - Goal of the first DM is a real reply, not a pitch — show me how each
     template earns that

3. THE BRIDGE
   - Give me 3 example DM exchanges (2-4 messages each) showing how a
     genuine reply-based conversation naturally arrives at "want me to send
     you [resource/offer]?" without it feeling like a script

4. WHAT NOT TO DO
   - List the 3 most common mistakes that make prospects go cold in this exact
     transition (comment → DM) and how to avoid each
```

## 7. Connection Request

**Goal:** Notes specific enough to earn acceptance before any pitch happens.

```
You are a LinkedIn outreach copywriter. My ICP is: {ICP}. I'm sending
connection requests to people who fit this profile, found via [content
engagers / hashtag search / group members / sales nav search — specify
which for each batch].

Write 8 connection request notes (under 300 characters each) split across
these approaches, 2 each:
1. Reference to a specific post/comment they made (templated with a
   placeholder for the specific detail)
2. Shared context (mutual group, similar content, shared connection)
3. Genuine compliment/observation about their content or business — specific,
   not flattery
4. Simple peer-to-peer note with zero pitch, zero offer mention

Rules:
- No mention of my offer, price, or "helping businesses like yours" in ANY
  of these — a connection note that pitches gets ignored or reported
- Written in {TONE}, sounds like a person, not a template
- After the list, tell me which type to use for which source (e.g. content
  engagers vs. cold Sales Nav search) and why
```

## 8. Outreach

**Goal:** First outbound message after connection, personalized enough to actually get replies.

```
You are a B2B outbound copywriter. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is
{YOUR_OFFER}. My proof: {PROOF}.

Write a first-touch outreach message (for LinkedIn DM, not email — keep it
short) with these requirements:

- Opens with something specific to them (their content, their business
  stage, a trigger event from my ICP research) — leave a clear [PERSONALIZATION]
  placeholder showing exactly what kind of detail goes there
- States a specific, narrow observation or insight relevant to bookkeeping
  firms at their revenue stage — not a generic compliment
- No pitch, no offer, no link in this first message
- Ends with a low-friction, specific question (not "interested in learning
  more?") that invites a real reply

Give me 3 variations:
1. Trigger-based (references a likely recent event — losing a client,
   hitting capacity, scaling struggles)
2. Insight-based (leads with a useful observation about their business)
3. Curiosity-based (references a result/pattern without explaining it yet)

For each, also write the FOLLOW-UP message to send if they reply with
interest but no commitment — the message that moves toward a call without
introducing a hard pitch.

Tone: {TONE}. Keep every message under 500 characters.
```

## 9. Lead Qualification

**Goal:** Filter before the calendar, not on the call.

```
You are a sales process designer for a B2B services business. My ICP is:
{ICP}. My offer is {YOUR_OFFER}, priced around {PRICE_POINT}.

Build a lead qualification framework I can run before anyone books a call:

1. QUALIFYING QUESTIONS (5-7)
   - Cover: revenue range, team size, current bottleneck, prior attempts to
     solve this, decision-making authority, timeline/urgency
   - Written as natural DM questions, not a survey — should feel like a
     real conversation, not an interrogation

2. SCORING RUBRIC
   - Turn the answers into a simple qualified / maybe / not-a-fit scoring
     system with clear criteria for each tier

3. DISQUALIFYING SIGNALS
   - List the 5 clearest signs someone is NOT a fit for {YOUR_OFFER} even
     if they're a bookkeeper in the right revenue range (e.g. wrong stage,
     price sensitivity red flags, no real authority to buy)

4. THE FILTER MESSAGE
   - Write the exact DM sequence that gathers this info naturally over 2-3
     messages before offering the calendar link — so unqualified leads
     self-select out before they ever see {CALENDAR_LINK}

5. WHAT TO DO WITH "MAYBE"
   - Give me a nurture path for leads who are close but not ready (what to
     send them, how often, what content to point them to)
```

## 10. Objection Handling

**Goal:** A reusable response matrix, not improvised answers every time.

```
You are a sales conversation strategist for B2B services sold to small
business owners. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is {YOUR_OFFER} at
{PRICE_POINT}. My proof: {PROOF}.

Build an objection-handling matrix for the objections bookkeeping firm
owners and solopreneurs are most likely to raise. For each objection, give
me: the underlying real concern behind it, a response framework (not a
script to recite word-for-word), and one example response in {TONE}.

Cover at minimum:
1. "I don't have the budget right now"
2. "I need to think about it"
3. "I've tried something like this before and it didn't work"
4. "I don't have time to implement this on top of client work"
5. "How is this different from [competitor/DIY approach]?"
6. "I want to talk to my [spouse/business partner] first"
7. "Can you just tell me the price first?"

For each: distinguish between a genuine objection and a stall — note which
type it usually is for this ICP and how the response should differ.

End with 3 general principles for objection handling with this specific
audience (bookkeepers tend to be detail-oriented, numbers-driven, and
skeptical of "marketing" pitches — build the guidance around that).
```

## 11. Follow-Up

**Goal:** A structured sequence, since most bookings come from the follow-up, not the first message.

```
You are a B2B follow-up sequence designer. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is
{YOUR_OFFER}.

Build a follow-up sequence for prospects who engaged (replied to outreach,
downloaded the lead magnet, or had a first conversation) but didn't book a
call or went quiet.

Give me a 5-touch sequence over ~14 days:
- Touch 1 (Day 1-2): [purpose + message]
- Touch 2 (Day 4-5): [purpose + message]
- Touch 3 (Day 7-8): [purpose + message]
- Touch 4 (Day 10-11): [purpose + message]
- Touch 5 (Day 14): [purpose + message — the "should I close this out" message]

Requirements:
- Each touch has a DIFFERENT job — do not repeat "just checking in" style
  messages; vary between value-add, social proof, direct ask, and
  permission-to-close
- At least one touch should share a relevant insight or resource, not just
  ask for their attention
- Write the final "breakup" message so it leaves the door open rather than
  sounding annoyed or final
- Keep each message short — this is DM, not email
- Tone: {TONE}

Also give me 2 signals that tell me to STOP the sequence early (reasons to
pause rather than keep pushing).
```

## 12. Closing

**Goal:** Move a warm conversation to a booked call — and the call to a decision — without a pitch-y pivot.

```
You are a sales conversation coach for consultative, low-pressure closing
in B2B services. My ICP is: {ICP}. My offer is {YOUR_OFFER} at
{PRICE_POINT}. My proof: {PROOF}.

1. DM-TO-CALL BRIDGE
   - Give me 3 ways to transition a warm DM conversation into a booked call
     that don't sound like "let's hop on a call" out of nowhere — each
     should tie the call to something specific the prospect said

2. CALL STRUCTURE
   - Outline a simple call flow (not a rigid script) for a discovery-to-close
     conversation with this ICP: opening, discovery questions, positioning
     {YOUR_OFFER} as the answer to what they said, handling the price
     conversation, and asking for the decision
   - Note where in this flow bookkeeping-specific concerns (cash flow
     awareness, ROI-mindedness, skepticism of "coaches") are most likely to
     surface and how to address them naturally within the flow

3. THE ASK
   - Give me 3 ways to ask for the close that fit {TONE} — direct without
     being pushy, and appropriate for a detail-oriented, numbers-driven
     buyer

4. IF THEY SAY "LET ME THINK ABOUT IT" ON THE CALL
   - Give me a response that surfaces the real hesitation without pressuring
     them, and a clear next step regardless of their answer

Keep the whole thing conversational — this should read like sales coaching
notes I can internalize, not a script I read off a page.
```
