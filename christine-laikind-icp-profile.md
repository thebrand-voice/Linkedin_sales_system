# ICP Profile — Christine Laikind
### Brand Foundation + Content Engine + Get Found Audit

**ICP:** Bookkeeping firm owners and solopreneur bookkeepers doing $250K–$1M in annual revenue

---

## 1. Firmographics

**Revenue band breakdown (this ICP isn't one buyer — it's a spectrum):**

| Sub-band | Profile |
|---|---|
| $250K–$450K | Often still solo or 1 contractor/VA. Owner is doing most of the client work personally. |
| $450K–$700K | Has a small team (1-3 bookkeepers/staff). Owner has stepped partway out of hands-on client work but is still deeply involved in delivery and sales. |
| $700K–$1M | Has a team of 3-6+, may have a manager layer. Owner's biggest constraint is usually visibility/lead flow, not capacity — they *can* serve more clients but aren't attracting the right ones. |

**Team structure:** Almost always started solo. Growth came from referrals and word of mouth, not marketing systems — which is exactly why they hit a ceiling here. Most have never had a real "brand" beyond a logo and a decent-looking website.

**Client base they serve:** Typically niche or semi-niche (e.g. e-commerce, agencies, contractors, med spas, real estate) or generalist local-market clients. The more niched firms in this range tend to have an easier time articulating their own positioning — and are often your best-fit buyers, because they already think in terms of "who I serve."

**Years in business:** 3-10 years is the sweet spot. Long enough to have real proof and client results, not long enough (or not intentional enough) to have built a brand that reflects it.

---

## 2. The Buyer

**How they actually describe themselves on LinkedIn (not job titles — headline language):**
- "Founder, [Firm Name] Bookkeeping"
- "Helping [niche] businesses get their books (and their lives) back"
- "Fractional CFO / Bookkeeper for [industry]"
- Solopreneurs often lead with credentials: "QuickBooks ProAdvisor | Xero Certified"

**Where they are in their journey when they become a fit for you:**
- Referrals have dried up or gotten inconsistent — they used to "not have to think about marketing" and now they do
- They've hit a revenue plateau for 1-2+ years despite doing good work
- They know they need to be visible online (usually specifically on LinkedIn, since that's where their referral partners and ideal clients are) but have no system — inconsistent posting, no strategy, embarrassed by their profile
- They may have tried posting sporadically, gotten no traction, and concluded "LinkedIn doesn't work for bookkeepers" — this is a real objection to plan for, not just a marketing problem to solve

**Daily bottlenecks specific to this stage:**
- Time: they're still billable, so "marketing" competes directly with paid client work — anything that requires a lot of their personal time will stall
- Confidence: many are excellent at the technical/financial side and genuinely uncomfortable with self-promotion — "I'm not a marketer" is a real internal barrier, not just an excuse
- No system: they know *what* they should probably be doing (post more, network more) but have no repeatable process, so it never becomes consistent

## 3. Buying Triggers

| Trigger | Urgency | What they're looking for |
|---|---|---|
| Referral pipeline slows for 2+ consecutive months | High | Immediate: "how do I get leads that don't depend on referrals" |
| Lost a large client (industry consolidation, client sold business, etc.) | High | Fast: need to backfill revenue, will move quickly if they trust you |
| Hit a revenue ceiling they can't explain (stuck at same number for a year+) | Medium-High | Diagnostic: want to understand *why*, receptive to an audit-style offer |
| Competitor or peer bookkeeper visibly "blowing up" on LinkedIn | Medium | Comparison-driven: "if they can do it, why can't I" — good hook territory |
| Just hired first team member / made a scaling investment | Medium | Growth-minded: wants lead flow to justify the new capacity |
| Rebrand trigger: new name, new niche focus, or leaving a franchise/network | Medium-High | Foundational: literally needs Brand Foundation work, not just content |
| Embarrassment moment: a prospect looked them up and their online presence didn't match their actual expertise | High, but often unspoken | Wants credibility fixed fast, sensitive/private about this one |

**Note on your specific offer:** the "Get Found Audit" piece maps almost perfectly to the "hit a ceiling, don't know why" and "embarrassment moment" triggers — those buyers want a diagnosis before a commitment, which makes the audit a strong door-opener even for people not ready for the full $4,500 package yet.

## 4. Where They Already Are

- LinkedIn is very likely their primary platform — bookkeepers skew toward LinkedIn over Instagram/TikTok because their referral partners (CPAs, financial advisors, attorneys) live there too
- Communities: Bookkeeper-specific Facebook groups (e.g. "Bookkeeper Business Launch" alumni communities, QuickBooks ProAdvisor groups, Xero community), plus niche-specific groups if they serve a vertical
- They follow other bookkeeping educators/influencers (people teaching pricing, niching, systems) more than general marketing influencers — they trust people who understand the profession specifically
- Hashtags/search terms: #bookkeeping, #virtualbookkeeper, #smallbusinessfinance, plus niche-specific tags
- Podcasts in this space tend to be bookkeeping-practice-management focused (pricing, niching, scaling a firm) — these audiences are worth watching for guest/engagement opportunities

## 5. Objections Before They Even Talk to You

1. **"I'm not a marketing person"** — deflects the whole category, not just your offer. Needs reassurance the system doesn't require them to become a content creator.
2. **"I tried posting before and nothing happened"** — burned by inconsistent, strategy-less effort. Needs to see this is a system, not "post more and hope."
3. **"$4,500 feels like a lot for something I could theoretically DIY"** — needs the time-cost math made explicit (what is their hour worth vs. building this alone over months).
4. **"How is this different from a generic branding agency?"** — they've likely seen generic agencies pitch generic small businesses. Needs the bookkeeping-specific positioning to land hard and fast.
5. **"Will this actually bring me the RIGHT clients, or just more noise?"** — this ICP has been burned by "more leads" promises before that brought unqualified tire-kickers. Speaks to their revenue/niche specificity, not volume.

## 6. Segment Split

**Segment A: "The Capped Solo"**
- $250K–$450K, still doing most delivery work personally
- Pain: time-starved, wants a system that doesn't require hours of weekly content creation
- Messaging angle: efficiency, "built once, works while you bill," low time investment
- Likely entry point: the Get Found Audit (lower commitment, diagnostic, fast win)

**Segment B: "The Stuck Firm Owner"**
- $450K–$1M, has a team, has stepped (partly) out of delivery
- Pain: has capacity to grow but the pipeline hasn't caught up — knows the ceiling is a visibility/brand problem, not a delivery problem
- Messaging angle: growth, positioning as the go-to name in their niche, ROI framed against team capacity sitting underused
- Likely entry point: ready for the full Brand Foundation + Content Engine package if trust is established

---

## 7. Qualifying Criteria

Before booking a call, check for:

1. **Revenue confirmation** — $250K-$1M annually (self-reported is fine; ask directly, bookkeepers respond well to direct numbers questions)
2. **Delivery model** — do they (or their team) serve clients in a repeatable way, or are they still figuring out their own service? (Pre-revenue or brand-new firms are not a fit yet)
3. **Referral dependency** — are they currently relying almost entirely on referrals/word of mouth for new clients? (Strong buying signal if yes)
4. **Some niche or point of view** — even loosely defined ("mostly e-commerce clients," "mostly contractors") — fully undifferentiated generalists need more foundational work before content strategy will land
5. **Decision authority** — solo owner, or firm owner who doesn't need partner/spouse sign-off for a $4,500 investment
6. **Real urgency signal present** — at least one buying trigger from section 3 has occurred in the last 1-3 months

**Score:**
- 5-6 yes → qualified, book directly
- 3-4 yes → maybe, nurture with content/audit offer first
- 0-2 yes → not a fit right now, add to long-term nurture list
