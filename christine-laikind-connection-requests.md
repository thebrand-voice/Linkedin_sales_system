# Connection Request Notes — Christine Laikind
### Brand Foundation + Content Engine + Get Found Audit
### ICP: Bookkeeping firm owners & solopreneur bookkeepers, $250K–$1M revenue

---

## 8 Connection Request Notes (under 300 characters each)

**Type 1: Reference to a specific post/comment they made** — for content engagers

**1.**
> Hey [Name] — saw your comment about [specific thing they said, e.g. "posting more without much traction"]. That's such a common spot for bookkeeping firm owners to get stuck. Would love to connect.

**2.**
> Hi [Name], your post about [specific topic] was a good read — especially the part about [specific detail]. Connecting so I can keep seeing what you're sharing.

---

**Type 2: Shared context** — for mutual groups, similar content, shared connections

**3.**
> Hey [Name] — noticed we're both in [group/community name]. Always good to connect with other people thinking seriously about the bookkeeping profession. Would love to add you.

**4.**
> Hi [Name], [Mutual Connection]'s posts are how I came across your profile. Looks like we run in similar circles — connecting so I can keep up with what you're building.

---

**Type 3: Genuine, specific compliment/observation**

**5.**
> Hey [Name] — your niche focus on [specific niche, e.g. "e-commerce clients"] is really clear from your profile. Not something a lot of bookkeeping firms nail. Would love to connect.

**6.**
> Hi [Name], I noticed you've been running [Firm Name] for [X years] — that kind of staying power in this industry says a lot. Connecting so I can follow along.

---

**Type 4: Simple peer-to-peer, zero pitch**

**7.**
> Hey [Name] — came across your profile and wanted to connect. Always good to grow my network with people in the bookkeeping/small business world.

**8.**
> Hi [Name], your work with bookkeeping firms/clients caught my eye. Would love to connect and follow what you're up to.

---

## Which Type to Use, and Why

**Content engagers (people who commented/liked your posts or the mini-audit):** Use **Type 1**. You already have a specific, genuine reason to reference — this earns the highest acceptance rate because it proves you actually saw them, not just their name in a list.

**Mutual groups / hashtag search / shared connections:** Use **Type 2**. Shared context lowers the stranger-danger feeling of a cold request without requiring you to have engaged with their content yet — useful for scaling beyond just your own post engagers.

**Cold Sales Navigator search (firms matching ICP criteria but no prior interaction):** Use **Type 3**. Since you have no engagement history to reference, a specific, researched observation about their profile (niche, tenure, positioning) is the next-best thing — it still proves you looked at them individually rather than mass-sending. This takes more manual research per request, so reserve it for your best-fit prospects, not high-volume outreach.

**High-volume, lower-research outreach batches:** Use **Type 4**. When you're sending in volume and can't personalize each one with research, keep it simple, honest, and pitch-free — it won't convert as high as Types 1-3, but it won't get you reported either, which a disguised pitch in the connection note risk doing.

---

## Reminder

None of these 8 notes mention the Audit, pricing, or "helping firms like yours" — that's intentional. A connection request that pitches, even softly, reads as sales and gets ignored or reported. The entire job of the connection note is to get accepted; the actual conversation happens after, using Prompt 8.
